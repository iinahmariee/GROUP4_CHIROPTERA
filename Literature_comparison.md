# Comparison with Published Phylogenetic Studies

Our phylogenetic tree clearly separates the fruit bats (*Pteropus vampyrus*, *Rousettus amplexicaudatus*, *Cynopterus brachyotis*) from the echolocating insectivorous bats (*Hipposideros diadema*, *Rhinolophus arcuatus*, *Miniopterus schreibersii*). This major division matches the established phylogenetic relationships reported by **Amador et al. (2018)**, which recognize the two main suborders: Yinpterochiroptera (fruit bats + rhinolophids) and Yangochiroptera.

The close pairing of *Hipposideros diadema* and *Rhinolophus arcuatus* (both in Superfamily Rhinolophoidea) is consistent with published classifications. Similarly, the clustering of *Pteropus vampyrus*, *Rousettus amplexicaudatus*, and *Cynopterus brachyotis* (all Family Pteropodidae) agrees with known taxonomic relationships. The outgroup *Mus musculus* branches separately from all bat species, confirming correct tree rooting.

Minor differences in exact branching order may occur because this study used only the COI barcoding gene, whereas published studies often use multiple genes or whole mitochondrial genomes. Despite this limitation, the COI gene successfully recovers the major evolutionary groups within the bats.
